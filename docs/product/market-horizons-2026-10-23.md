# KRUXT Market Horizons

Research cutoff and access date: **2026-10-23**. Internal decision research, not a
market-size forecast, investment recommendation or claim of product-market fit.

## Decision In Brief

**Inference:** KRUXT's strongest initial demand hypothesis is affordable,
coach-supported training continuity inside a real local community. Start with an
Italian strength/hybrid gym cluster, but test a separate beginner/older-adult
cohort before generalizing from enthusiastic competitors. The evidence supports
investigating these needs, not launching every business model at once. Evidence
anchors: Italian participation [S1-S3], EU barriers [S4-S5], sector economics
[S6-S7, S10-S11], and professional trend observations [S8-S9], dated below.

**Recommendation:** make private training useful first; test whether optional
people, shared events and coach follow-up add repeat participation and economic
value. Treat AI as a supervised productivity hypothesis, wearables as a
permission-bound convenience, and creator reach as a costed service. A global
feed, autonomous coaching and universal performance scores are not no-regret bets.

Builds on the existing [competitive strategy](competitive-strategy-2026-10-22.md)
and [social/gamification brief](social-gamification-brief.md), both read for context.
This report adds demand, economics and time horizons; it does not repeat vendor
feature comparisons or change their delivery/security gates.

## Method And Evidence Labels

- **Observed (O):** a dated source reports a measured result, company result or
  survey response. A published expectation is observed *as an expectation*, not
  an achieved outcome. Survey rankings are not consumer purchase decisions.
- **Inference (I):** KRUXT interpretation of cited observations; unvalidated until
  tested with its actual participants and buyers.
- **Scenario (S):** a plausible conditional future, not a prediction or probability.
- **Gap (G):** evidence not established in this research. Missing evidence is not
  evidence of absence.

Only sources published by the cutoff are admitted. Publication year, observation
period and access date are different. The 2027 ACSM release is admissible because
it was published **2026-10-20** [S9]; no unpublished 2027 market report is assumed.
Use the 2026 Eurobarometer rather than presenting its 2022 predecessor as current.
Research used free primary releases, a public report extract, original research
and issuer disclosures. No paid report, outreach, account signup or purchase.

## Observed Evidence Ledger

### Italy: Initial Context

**S1 / O - Participation, training context and digital use.** ISTAT's detailed
2024 study, published **2025-06-30**, reports sport participation at 37.5% among
people aged 3+, with 28.7% continuous participation. Among sports participants,
48.8% train once or twice weekly; 18.7% use fitness apps, social networks or
specialist websites; 45.6% have in-person/remote instruction. Indoor facilities
are used by 59.5%; home/condominium participation reached 20.2%, versus 13.5% in
2015. These settings can overlap. Sport participation is 23.3% at ages 65-74 and
8.1% at 75+. The North-East reports 43.9%, versus 27.9% in southern/island areas.
These are sport measures, not paid-gym or app subscriptions.
[ISTAT detailed report, pp. 1-4](https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/La-pratica-sportiva-in-Italia_2024.pdf).

**S2 / O - Newer national direction, not a replacement for every S1 detail.**
ISTAT's **2026-04-23** historical overview includes 2025 data: continuous sport
participation is 30.3%, and sport including occasional participation is nearly
40%. It identifies a roughly 11-point Centre-North/Mezzogiorno gap. This is a
newer headline observation; do not silently relabel S1's technology, setting or
age breakdowns as 2025 measurements or splice surveys into a precise growth rate.
[ISTAT, "Tra cultura e svago", sport section](https://www.istat.it/produzione-editoriale/cultura-svago/).

**S3 / O - Aging is already present.** ISTAT, published **2026-03-31**, estimates
14.821 million residents aged 65+ at 2026-01-01, or 25.1% of the population;
figures are provisional. Age is not equivalent to capacity, digital proficiency,
interest in coaching or ability to pay.
[ISTAT demographic indicators for 2025](https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/indicatori-demografici-anno-2025/).

### Europe: Demand And Economics

**S4 / O - A genuinely current EU survey exists.** The European Commission's
2026 Sport and Physical Activity Eurobarometer was released **2026-09-22**, DOI
10.2766/2345953. Its publication record establishes scope and availability, not an
Italian gym demand estimate.
[EU Publications Office report record](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/bc4cba9e-b62b-11f1-81de-01aa75ed71a1/language-en).

**S5 / O - Participation improved, constraints remain.** The Commission's
**2026-09-22** summary reports EU-wide never-sport/exercise responses falling
from 45% in 2022 to 39% in 2026; 38% exercise several times weekly. Barriers to
more sport/physical activity include time (37%), motivation/interest (26%) and
price (12%). It reports 26,421 EU-27 respondents, fieldwork in April 2026.
These are self-reports, not transactions; EU averages do not identify Italian
price elasticity. Do not confuse sport/exercise with all everyday movement.
[European Commission representation, survey summary](https://belgium.representation.ec.europa.eu/actualites-et-evenements/actualites/semaine-europeenne-du-sport-les-belges-et-les-europeens-bougent-plus-quavant-2026-09-22_fr).

**S6 / O - European clubs grew in 2025.** EuropeActive's release is dated
**2026-04-22**, with an announcement dateline of **2026-04-15**: 75.5 million
memberships, up 5.8%, and EUR 39.1 billion revenue, up 9.1%, partly from higher
yield/member. It reports 67,515 clubs. The body inconsistently calls the report
"2025"; the linked edition and Deloitte extract identify **2026**, covering 2025.
One top-20 membership sentence has an obvious currency/unit error; it is not used.
An association's 100-million-by-2030 ambition is not an independent forecast.
[EuropeActive/Deloitte public release](https://www.europeactive.eu/blog/press-corner-4/strong-growth-in-members-and-revenues-for-european-health-fitness-market-in-2025-140).

**S7 / O - Consolidation and heterogeneous preferences.** Deloitte's public
**2026 edition**, launched April 2026, corroborates the market totals and records
27 qualifying operator M&A transactions transferring 936 clubs during 2025.
Its consumer takeaways distinguish younger digital/specialist-format preferences
from older cohorts' more traditional formats. The extract does not provide the
complete sampling/methodology or Italian willingness-to-pay tables. "Regular
fitness" survey participation is not the same denominator as club membership,
ISTAT sport or Eurobarometer exercise.
[Deloitte/EuropeActive public extract, PDF pp. 3-6](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone2/de/de/docs/industries/consumer/2026/Auszug-European_Health_and_Fitness_Market_Report_2026.pdf).

### Wider Signals: Do Not Treat As Italian Market Estimates

**S8 / O - 2026 professional priorities.** ACSM's release, **2025-10-22**, reports
a survey of about 2,000 professionals. Wearables rank first, older-adult programs
second and mobile exercise apps fourth; adult recreation/sport clubs enter the
top trends. This is professional observation/forecasting, not a representative
Italian consumer panel or evidence that KRUXT improves adherence.
[ACSM 2026 trends announcement](https://acsm.org/top-fitness-trends-2026/).

**S9 / O - 2027 trends were actually published before this cutoff.** ACSM's
**2026-10-20** release reports more than 1,400 professional respondents rating
50 trends. Wearables rank first, mobile apps second, older-adult programs fourth,
traditional strength fifth, group classes seventh and influencer-led programs
tenth. Group classes rose from thirteenth. The release explicitly warns that
ranking does not establish effectiveness. Different respondents and ranks do not
measure market growth or prove a worldwide shift toward a single format.
[ACSM 2027 trends announcement](https://acsm.org/2027-fitness-trends/).

**S10 / O - Scale does not make revenue equivalent to cash or profit.** Basic-Fit's
2025 annual report, available in **2026** (exact release day not verified here),
reports combined group revenue of EUR 1,420 million, underlying EBITDA less rent
of EUR 348 million and net profit of EUR 14.3 million. Its 5.8 million memberships
include owned and franchised Clever Fit locations. Acquisition/perimeter changes
matter; these figures are not an independent Italian gym margin benchmark.
[Basic-Fit 2025 annual report, results and definitions](https://annualreport.basic-fit.com/2025/index.html).

**S11 / O - Recent operator results show demand and operating discipline.**
PureGym's issuer-authored **2026-08-27** H1 release reports revenue of GBP 393.0m
(+6%), adjusted EBITDA of GBP 123.6m (+23%), and 2.442 million members (+3%).
It attributes growth to expansion, membership and revenue/member changes, and
discusses cost management and US integration. Its adjusted EBITDA is not net
profit, and cannot be compared directly with Basic-Fit's rent-adjusted measure.
These are multi-country chain outcomes, not independent-gym software budgets.
[PureGym H1 2026 issuer RNS 3239S, reproduced by London South East](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/puregym-2026-half-year-results-5ulnrpwnh24f0ph.html?mobile_view=desktop).

**S12 / O - AI acceptance evidence is narrow.** Wachholz et al., published
**2025-03-13**, studied 119 German-speaking recreational athletes recruited around
Innsbruck, mean age 28.5, using January 2024 survey data, plus six coach interviews.
AI-plan users reported greater trust than non-users. The plan comparison concerned
beginner half-marathon preparation. Selection, age, geography and task constrain
transfer; neither trust nor difficulty distinguishing authors demonstrates safe
autonomous strength coaching, clinical benefit or Italian demand.
[Original BMC Research Notes pilot, methods and results](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-025-07172-9).

**S13 / O - Adaptive prompting has some experimental support, not a general AI
coaching proof.** PEARL, accepted **2026-08-25**, is presented as an accepted,
not-yet-copyedited manuscript on JMIR Preprints. It randomized 13,463 Fitbit users;
7,711 entered the primary analysis, 86.3% female. At two months the adaptive arm
reported +210 daily steps versus control. Attrition/analysis exclusions and short
follow-up limit interpretation. This tests step-oriented nudges, not generated
lifting prescriptions, social feeds, gym retention or willingness to pay.
[Original PEARL trial abstract and publication status](https://preprints.jmir.org/preprint/91156).

**S14 / O - Hybrid competition has a commercial demand signal.** PUMA's
**2025-10-01** sponsor release reports 74 HYROX events and over 650,000 participants
for 2024/25. Its 1.3-million figure for 2025/26 is explicitly an expectation,
not a verified outcome in this report. Sponsor-reported participants may not be
unique people; neither this count nor a global calendar establishes Italy's
addressable audience or repeat demand for KRUXT.
[PUMA/HYROX partnership announcement](https://about.puma.com/en/newsroom/corporate-news/2025/01-10-2025-puma-announces-early-renewal-its-long-term-partnership).

**S15 / O - Creator credibility needs operational support.** UNESCO's
**2024-11-26** release, updated **2024-11-27**, describes a survey of 500 creators
in 45 countries: 62% did not perform rigorous systematic fact-checking and 73%
wanted training. This is not a representative fitness-creator prevalence estimate,
nor evidence that Italian coaches publish unsafe material. It supports asking
about sourcing, incentives and support rather than treating popularity as expertise.
[UNESCO original survey release](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/2/3-digital-content-creators-do-not-check-their-facts-sharing-want-learn-how-do-so-unesco-survey?hub=701).

**S16 / O - Interoperability is permissioned, not frictionless.** Google's dated
**2025-03-12** Health Connect announcement describes on-device app data sharing,
separate background/history permissions, revocation and feature-availability
checks. It is historical platform evidence, not a current API implementation
specification or a guarantee all wearable vendors expose equivalent data.
[Android Developers platform announcement](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/03/health-connect-jetpack-sdk-now-in-beta.html).

### Geographic Transfer Rules

**Italy first (I):** select a reachable local cluster on observed partner/member
tasks, not national population totals. Include non-posters, women, beginners,
older adults and people declining device permissions in research. Deliberately
test outside the initial affluent/urban enthusiast group before national rollout.
S1-S3 justify segmentation, not choosing a winning city or assuming every older
adult wants an app. G: no city-level gym budgets or KRUXT acquisition costs.

**Other Europe (I):** validate language, price, coaching practice, local schedules,
procurement and support cost country by country. S6-S7 include Europe beyond the
EU-27; S4-S5 concern the EU-27. Do not compare their participation percentages
as if the geographical coverage and definitions were identical.

**Wider market (I):** use ACSM, the trial and sponsor signals to generate tasks
worth testing, not to extrapolate adoption. US/global platform distribution,
event economics and coaching markets need separate local research. No global
TAM, penetration catch-up assumption or revenue CAGR is warranted here.

## No-Regret Bets And Defensibility Experiments

**Recommendations, not market findings:** preserve a private logging path,
exportability, accessible text-first flows, optional sensors/social, explicit
audiences, versioned coach content and measurable service cost in every scenario.
"No-regret" means useful across these scenarios, not free of implementation cost.

Potential defensibility is earned through repeat local relationships, dependable
user-controlled history, coach workflow knowledge and a credible operating record.
It is not established by adding AI, video or badges. Test the mechanisms below.

### Measurement Contract Before Any Pilot

No pilot or data collection is executed or newly authorized by this report.
Owner-approved recruitment, privacy/safety and existing release gates come first.

- Define **weekly active training athlete** as one distinct consenting participant
  with at least one deliberately completed eligible session in the week. Label
  self-report as self-report; deduplicate attendance, booking and workout records.
- Measure first useful completion, week-4/week-8 repeat activity, participant-rated
  usefulness/belonging, paid continuation and net staff/creator minutes. Count all
  invited people in conversion denominators; report opt-outs and missing data.
- Predeclare comparison cohorts or staggered invitation by group. Avoid attributing
  gym-wide renewal differences causally to KRUXT from a small uncontrolled pilot.
  Group-level assignment helps limit social spillover; keep seasonal context.
- For scale decisions, define the minimum worthwhile effect and sample requirement
  from baseline data before viewing results. Report uncertainty intervals. Small
  pilots establish feasibility, not population efficacy; inconclusive means no scale.
- Track saves lost, audience leaks, unwanted contact, unsafe-content reports,
  time-to-triage, appeals and moderator/support hours. Do not reward exertion volume,
  weight change, compulsive streaks or disclosure of health information.

The numeric thresholds below are **proposed management gates**, not published
benchmarks or forecasts. Confirm their feasibility with the owner before a test;
do not move a failed threshold after inspecting results. Each "kill" stops that
bet's expansion, not the participant's access to their records or support.

### Economics To Compute From Actual Pilot Records

These are proposed accounting definitions, not estimates of current KRUXT costs.

**KRUXT contribution per gym/cohort/month** = recognized net revenue minus
processing/refunds, media processing/storage/delivery, moderation, support,
onboarding allocation, coach/content subsidies, model inference/review and
integration maintenance. Record staff/founder time at an explicit replacement
cost. Separate contribution from company profit and cash runway.

**Gym value case** = demonstrable staff time saved plus incremental contribution
from attended paid services/renewals that can reasonably be attributed, minus
KRUXT fees, staff effort and transition costs. Attendance is not automatically
incremental revenue; filling an already-full class may add no contribution.

**Creator net return** = actual attributable receipts minus fees/refunds,
production time and moderation/support time. Views, plan saves and hypothetical
future earnings do not count as receipts. **Acquisition payback** uses fully
loaded acquisition/onboarding cost divided by positive monthly contribution;
when contribution is nonpositive, payback is undefined, not a favorable ratio.

Do not multiply EUR 39.1bn sector revenue by an assumed capture percentage.
No researched source establishes KRUXT's reachable sites, conversion, renewal,
software budget, pricing power or gross margin. Chain adjusted earnings and
nominal market growth are context, not inputs to a fabricated SaaS forecast.
